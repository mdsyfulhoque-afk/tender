# MASTER PROMPT — Convert This Entire Discussion into a Final DOCX

Using the **entire discussion in this chat as the source material**, prepare one complete, polished, professional DOCX that consolidates everything we developed, decided, refined, corrected, and finalized during the conversation.

## 1. Primary Objective

Do not merely summarize the conversation.

Transform the discussion into a **standalone final document** that someone could use without reading the chat history.

The document must capture:

- the original objective;
- all important requirements;
- all decisions and refinements;
- all frameworks, methodologies, workflows, prompts, templates, examples, formulas, instructions, checklists, and operating rules developed during the discussion;
- all corrections and later improvements that supersede earlier versions;
- any pilot-run or implementation procedure;
- expected outputs;
- quality assurance requirements;
- limitations and assumptions;
- final reusable prompts or instructions;
- implementation guidance;
- appendices or reusable templates where useful.

Treat later confirmed decisions as authoritative when they conflict with earlier discussion.

---

# 2. Reconstruct the Discussion Before Writing

Before creating the DOCX:

1. Review the **full conversation from beginning to end**.
2. Identify:
   - original problem;
   - evolving requirements;
   - user preferences;
   - rejected or superseded ideas;
   - accepted final decisions;
   - examples created;
   - reusable prompts;
   - workflows;
   - deliverables;
   - QA requirements;
   - unresolved issues.
3. Build an internal hierarchy of what belongs in the final document.
4. Remove conversational repetition, filler, acknowledgements, false starts, and duplicated material.
5. Preserve substantive detail.
6. Do not omit useful content merely because it appeared early in the discussion.
7. If later discussion improves an earlier section, use the improved version rather than reproducing both unless the evolution itself is important.

---

# 3. Document Philosophy

The final DOCX must be:

- **self-contained**;
- **implementation-ready**;
- **professionally structured**;
- **editable**;
- **easy to navigate**;
- **detailed enough to function as a manual, blueprint, handbook, specification, or operating document**;
- written so that another AI, colleague, consultant, developer, analyst, researcher, or future version of me could understand and use it without needing the original chat.

Do not produce a superficial meeting summary.

Convert informal conversation into formal documentation.

# 10. Handling Inconsistencies in the Discussion

If the conversation contains conflicting instructions:

1. Prefer the **latest explicit user instruction**.
2. Preserve any earlier instruction that remains compatible.
3. Do not silently merge contradictory requirements.
4. Where a conflict materially affects the final document, include a short note explaining the final adopted rule.
5. Do not revive an idea the user explicitly rejected.

Use professional handbook/manual/report language.

- chatty dialogue;

- excessive references to the conversation itself;
- filler;

- generic AI language;
- unsupported claims;
- unnecessary promotional wording.

Prefer:

- clear headings;
- concise paragraphs;
- structured tables;
- numbered procedures;
- implementation-oriented language;
- explicit assumptions;
